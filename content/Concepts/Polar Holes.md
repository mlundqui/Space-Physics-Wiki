# Polar Holes

Regions of the polar cap with anomalously low electron density — the opposite of [[Polar Cap Patch|polar cap patches]]. Defined by a peak electron density below $N_{mF2} < 4\times10^{10}$ m$^{-3}$ ($f_{oF2} < 1.8$ MHz). Satellite in-situ measurements have recorded densities as low as $2\times10^8$ m$^{-3}$ — so low that HF skywave propagation is impossible and most ground-based instruments (ionosondes, GPS TEC, ISR) cannot detect the structure at all.

## Formation

Polar holes form when F-region plasma recombines without being replenished. Two contributing classes of mechanism are proposed:

**Stagnant dark convection cells.** In a two-cell convection pattern the electrostatic potential $\Phi$ has local extrema where $\nabla\Phi = 0$ and $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B} = 0$. Potential contours immediately around these stagnant points trace small closed orbits. If these orbits remain in perpetual darkness (straightforward in winter, when the solar terminator is equatorward of the polar cap), the trapped flux tubes lose O$^+$ steadily through the two-step loss $\text{O}^+ + \text{N}_2 \rightarrow \text{NO}^+ + \text{N}$ and $\text{NO}^+ + e \rightarrow \text{N} + \text{O}$, with no photoionization to compensate.

**Locally accelerated recombination.** The same loss-enhancement mechanisms proposed for [[Polar Cap Patch|patch cutting]] — fast convection channels elevating $T_i$ and therefore the rate coefficients $k_1$, $k_2$; localised N$_2$ density enhancements; or downward neutral winds that push O$^+$ into the molecular-rich lower thermosphere — can accelerate loss and create density voids without requiring stagnant convection.

Makarevich et al. found that polar holes observed by [[RISR-N]] show no preference for fast or slow convection speeds, and Forsythe et al. used multi-instrument flux-tube tracing to show that holes do not require flux tubes to be trapped on stagnant convection cells. This points to locally accelerated recombination as a necessary ingredient, not just stagnation.

## Relationship to patches and instabilities

The density gradient at the edges of polar holes, like the trailing edge of [[Polar Cap Patch|patches]], is potentially unstable to the [[Ionospheric Instabilities|gradient-drift instability]]. Simulations confirm that holes can drive GDI. Because hole boundaries create their own density-gradient-driven turbulence, polar holes are also associated with HF scintillation and backscatter — the same radio consequences as patches, but driven by density depletions rather than enhancements.

## Observation challenges

Ground-based techniques (ionosondes, GPS TEC, ISR) lack the dynamic range to detect densities as low as $2\times10^8$ m$^{-3}$. The most extreme observations were made by in-situ satellite probes (Brinton et al., Dynamics Explorer era). Polar holes are therefore substantially under-studied compared to patches: the same instrument networks used to build patch climatologies cannot detect deep holes.
