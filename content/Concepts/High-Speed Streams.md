# High-Speed Streams (CH/HSS)

Fast solar wind originating from coronal holes (CHs) — open-field regions in the solar corona. The interface between a high-speed stream and the slower wind ahead of it forms a **stream interaction region (SIR)** with compressed plasma, enhanced IMF magnitude, and Alfvénic fluctuations.

## Geoeffectiveness

CH/HSS conditions can produce **extended intervals of moderate to high geomagnetic activity** (high AE, AU, AL) even without driving deeply negative SYM-H (Denton & Borovsky 2012; Grandin et al. 2019). The IMF Bz under HSS typically fluctuates in sign without a clear polarity preference (Kilpua et al. 2017), so CH/HSS-driven activity is geomagnetically active *without* a clean southward-IMF signature.

CH/HSS occurrence peaks during the declining phase and solar-minimum portions of the solar cycle.

## Why this matters for the polar cap

[[Lundquist Varney 2026]] identifies **L events** (anomalously lifted [[F-Layer|F2]] peaks at [[RISR-N]] that are *not* dense) as a population whose driver signature matches CH/HSS — fast solar wind, high AE/AU/AL and dynamic pressure, no strong correlation with negative Bz or SYM-H, and lower median F10.7 than the full database (consistent with declining-phase / solar-minimum HSS frequency). This is a previously unidentified ionospheric phenomenon in the patch literature; understanding *why* CH/HSS preferentially produces lifted (but not necessarily dense) F-region structures is an open question.

## Solar Wind Origin

Fast solar wind from coronal holes is the high-temperature / high-velocity solution of the Parker transonic outflow (see [[Parker 1958 Solar Wind]]). Parker's analysis showed that the solar corona at ~$10^6$ K cannot be in static equilibrium and must continuously expand supersonically; the asymptotic velocity scales with the coronal temperature at the sonic point. Coronal hole regions — with open magnetic field and lower electron densities — produce faster, less dense wind than the slow wind from streamer belt regions. The frozen-in solar magnetic field is drawn into the Parker spiral; HSS events carry enhanced Alfvénic fluctuations that drive geomagnetic activity through southward B_z fluctuations even without a strong southward IMF bias. *(Caveat added 2026-10-08: an isothermal Parker wind does give faster wind for a hotter corona, but coronal holes are not hotter than streamers. Realistic fast-wind speeds need extra heating and momentum deposited above the critical point, e.g. by Alfvén waves. See [[Parker Solar Wind and Spiral]], "What we assumed".)*
