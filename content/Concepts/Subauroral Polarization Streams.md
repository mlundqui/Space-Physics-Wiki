# Subauroral Polarization Streams

**SAPS** — a narrow channel of fast westward (sunward at 18 MLT) ion drift in the sub-auroral ionosphere, located between the equatorward edge of the diffuse electron precipitation oval and the plasmapause. First systematically characterized as a new category of sub-auroral electric field by Foster & Burke (2002).

## Physical mechanism

The SAPS electric field arises from a **low-conductance gap** between the inner edge of the [[Ring Current|ring current]] electron precipitation and the inner edge of the Region-2 current closure:

1. Region-2 field-aligned currents (downward on the dusk side) close through the ionosphere equatorward of the auroral precipitation boundary.
2. In the sub-auroral ionosphere (below the precipitation), Pedersen conductance is low (no precipitation ionization source).
3. The closure of Region-2 current through this low-conductance region builds up a strong **poleward electric field** ($E \approx 10$–100 mV/m at peak).
4. The poleward E field drives fast westward $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}$ drift — the SAPS flow — at velocities of hundreds to $\sim 2000$ m/s.

From [[Bao 2023 Geospace Plume MAGE]] (MAGE simulation of 31 March 2001 superstorm): the SAPS velocity peaked at **~2000 m/s** at 07:03 UT during the late main phase. The SAPS location during this storm was at 45–50 MLAT, shifted equatorward compared to the typical 52–64 MLAT range (Foster & Vo 2002), due to the extreme Kp / ring current injection depth.

## Effects

### Plasmasphere erosion
The sunward ion flux in the SAPS channel at the dusk edge of the plasmasphere transports cold plasma sunward and away from the magnetosphere, eroding the plasmapause and forming the **plasmaspheric drainage plume** — the cold plasma counterpart to the ionospheric SED plume. Together they form the **geospace plume** ([[Bao 2023 Geospace Plume MAGE]], [[Foster 2020 Geospace Plume]]).

### Ionospheric TEC trough
SAPS-driven westward transport removes O$^+$ from the sub-auroral ionosphere faster than recombination or production can replenish it, creating the **mid-latitude electron density trough** (co-located with the SAPS channel). Frictional heating from the fast ion drift also accelerates recombination via elevated $T_{eff}$ (Schunk et al. 1976).

### SAPS vs. Convection Return Flow (CRF)
During storm main phase, two westward ion drift (WID) channels coexist at 18 MLT (Bao 2023):
- **SAPS** (midlatitude, ~45–55 MLAT): overlaps the plasmapause; primary erosion mechanism.
- **CRF** (higher latitude, ~57–65 MLAT): associated with the closure of Region-1/2 convection; does not interact with the plasmasphere.

## Observations

SAPS are routinely observed by DMSP (cross-track ion drift at 840 km), SuperDARN (HF coherent backscatter), and incoherent scatter radars (PFISR, [[RISR-N]]). The co-location of the SAPS velocity peak with the electron density trough and the plasmasphere boundary is a key observational constraint.

DMSP F13 and F15 observations from the 31 March 2001 storm (Bao 2023, Fig. 9): SAPS velocity ~2000 m/s collocated with acute density trough and plasmasphere boundary at ~48 MLAT.
