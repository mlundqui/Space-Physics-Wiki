# Ionosphere

The partially ionized portion of Earth's upper atmosphere, sustained by EUV and X-ray photoionization on the dayside and (at high latitudes) by particle precipitation, and depleted by recombination chemistry. Conventionally divided by altitude into D, E, and F regions; the [[F-Layer]] holds the electron-density peak. The ionosphere is the medium through which HF radio waves propagate and the lower boundary of the magnetosphere-ionosphere-thermosphere coupled system.

At F-region altitudes the plasma is nearly collisionless, so horizontal transport is dominated by **$E\times B$ drift**. Vertical transport is more complicated: parallel ion velocities are set by a competition between gravity, parallel pressure gradients, ambipolar electric fields, and ion-neutral drag from the parallel projection of the neutral wind (Kelley 2009). The geomagnetic field is tilted, so even a "horizontal" $E\times B$ drift on the dayside has an upwards component — relevant to [[Lifting|lifting]] and to [[Storm-Enhanced Density|SED]] formation.
