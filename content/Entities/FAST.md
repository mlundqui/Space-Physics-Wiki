# FAST

**Fast Auroral SnapshoT** Explorer: a NASA Small Explorer in a high-inclination elliptical orbit (about 350–4175 km altitude), designed to sample at high resolution the region where auroral electrons are accelerated. It flew through the bottom of the auroral acceleration region with high-cadence fields (electric field, fluxgate and search-coil magnetometers) and particle analyzers (electrons and ions, 4 eV–32 keV, full pitch-angle coverage, sub-second resolution; see [[Carlson 2001 FAST Plasma Instrument]]).

## Key science contributions in this wiki

- **Three auroral current regions.** One pass resolves inverted-V (upward current), return current (downward) and Alfvénic (mixed) regions. Basis of the textbook framework in [[Strangeway Ch11 The Aurora]].
- **Horseshoe distributions and the Knight relation** in the acceleration region; AKR generation in the about 0.3 cm⁻³ density cavity ([[Strangeway Ch11 The Aurora]]).
- **Dispersive Alfvén wave statistics** ([[Chaston 2003 FAST Small-Scale Alfvén Waves]]):
  - DAWs occur throughout the oval, most often in the cusp and most intensely at the premidnight polar-cap boundary.
  - Median current widths are about 1 km mapped to the ionosphere.
  - Most Alfvénic electron acceleration happens *above* FAST apogee, at 1–2 $R_E$.
- **Auroral energy budget** ([[Chaston 2007 DAW Auroral Acceleration Fraction]]): DAWs carry 25–39% of electron energy deposition and 15–34% of energetic ion outflow, from more than 5000 polar passes (1996–1999).

## Limitations

- The orbit lies mostly *below* the main Alfvénic acceleration altitude, so Alfvén-accelerated electrons can appear at FAST with almost no dissipative-scale waves present (strong reflection from the $v_A$ gradient on the nightside).
- The 30 keV upper energy limit misses the high-energy diffuse tail.
