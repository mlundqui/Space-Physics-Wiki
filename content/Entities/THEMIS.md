# THEMIS

The Time History of Events and Macroscale Interactions during Substorms — a five-spacecraft NASA constellation launched February 17, 2007. Designed to resolve the decades-long controversy over substorm onset origin: near-Earth current disruption vs. distant magnetotail reconnection. Each probe carries identical instrument suites including a fluxgate magnetometer (FGM), electrostatic analyzer, solid-state telescope, search coil magnetometer, and electric field instrument.

## Orbit and Configuration

THEMIS probes follow elliptical orbits with perigee ~1 R_E and apogee from 10 R_E (inner probes) to 30 R_E (outer probe). The orbital configuration enables simultaneous multi-point measurements spanning the inner magnetosphere, outer magnetosphere, and solar wind. After the primary mission, two probes (THEMIS-B and THEMIS-C) were reassigned to lunar orbit — renamed ARTEMIS — for lunar plasma wake and magnetotail science.

## FGM Instrument

Described by [[Auster 2007 THEMIS FGM]]:
- Sensitivity: 0.01 nT; frequency range: DC to 64 Hz
- Single sensor on compact 2-m boom; digital fluxgate technology (onboard software replaces analog hardware)
- Covers solar wind, magnetosheath, magnetotail, and outer magnetosphere environments
- In-flight calibration: UCLA group using spin-tone Fourier analysis

Companion calibration methodology (applied to Van Allen Probe magnetometers): [[Vasquez 2020 Van Allen Probe FGM Calibration]].

## Scientific Results

**Substorm onset:** Multi-probe timing resolved substorm onset within 2 R_E of Earth, demonstrating that current disruption precedes distant tail reconnection — settling a ~20-year debate.

**Upstream solar wind monitoring:** THEMIS outer probe provides solar wind B and plasma data for conjunction studies with ground-based instruments. Upstream IMF southward turning times from THEMIS are used to lag-correlate with auroral onset and convection response at RISR-N and SuperDARN.

**Connection to high-latitude ionosphere:** Substorm injections observed by THEMIS inner probes connect to auroral precipitation, [[NEIALs]], and polar cap patch breakup at the open-closed field-line boundary. Solar wind and IMF data from THEMIS outer probe drive convection models (Weimer, AMIE) used in polar cap patch transport studies.
