# Olifer 2023 KP Self-Limiting Precipitation

*Intense Energetic Electron Precipitation Caused by the Self-Limiting of Space Radiation.* Olifer, L., et al. (2023). *Geophysical Research Letters*, 50, e2023GL105392. doi:10.1029/2023GL105392.

## Summary

Superposed epoch analysis of 54 keV electron flux during 70 geomagnetic storms (Dst ≤ −50 nT, 2012–2019) using Van Allen Probes (equatorial, 90° pitch angle) and POES satellites (low Earth orbit, precipitating flux) demonstrates that the most intense electron precipitation events are directly linked to the self-limiting Kennel-Petschek (K-P) process. Dawn-side electron injections briefly drive fluxes above the K-P limit, triggering chorus wave growth that rapidly returns fluxes to the K-P asymptotic level; this self-limiting drives intense precipitation seen by POES at LEO. The K-P limit defines the entire pitch-angle distribution (PAD) shape, not just the equatorial flux.

## Key Claims

1. **Most intense precipitation is caused by dawn-side substorm electron injections** that briefly raise fluxes above the K-P limit, exciting chorus waves that scatter electrons to the loss cone.
2. **Flux capping at the K-P limit** is demonstrated observationally: both trapped (Van Allen Probes) and precipitating (POES) fluxes converge to the same asymptotic level across all storms, at L ≈ 4.5 this is ~5×10$^3$ cm$^{-2}$ s$^{-1}$ sr$^{-1}$ keV$^{-1}$.
3. **Dusk-side fluxes are always at or just below K-P levels** — flux variability collapses to factor of 2 near storm onset, consistent with K-P regulation rather than injection dynamics.
4. **The K-P process shapes the entire PAD** from 90° (equatorial) to 0° (loss cone), not just a boundary condition on the equatorial flux.
5. **Strong chorus wave growth** is required for the fast (sub-day) scattering; nonlinear interactions are not ruled out by this observational study.

## Methods/Data

- Van Allen Probes (RBSP): MagEIS instrument, 54 keV electron flux at 90° pitch angle.
- POES: Medium Energy Proton and Electron Detector (MEPED), 30–100 keV channel; 90° detector (trapped) and 0° detector (precipitating).
- Superposed epoch: 70 storms, epoch = minimum Dst ≤ −50 nT; dawn (3–9 MLT) and dusk (15–21 MLT) sectors analyzed separately.
- K-P limit estimated from Van Allen Probe fluxes at storm onset asymptote.

## Open Questions

- Whether nonlinear (as opposed to quasilinear) chorus wave interactions are required to explain the fast scattering rates.
- The MLT asymmetry mechanism: why does dusk-side remain capped while dawn-side fluctuates above the limit.
