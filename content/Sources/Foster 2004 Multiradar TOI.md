# Foster 2004 Multiradar TOI

*Multiradar observations of the polar tongue of ionization.* Foster, J.C., et al. (2005). *Journal of Geophysical Research*, 110, A09S31. doi:10.1029/2004JA010928.

## Summary

Multi-instrument case study of the polar tongue of ionization (TOI) during the 20 November 2003 super-storm (Dst min ~−472 nT). Simultaneously observing the complete SED→cusp→polar cap→nightside auroral boundary chain with three ISRs, SuperDARN HF radars, DMSP satellites, and GPS TEC maps reveals the TOI as a continuous stream of cold SED plasma entrained in the global convection pattern.

## Key Claims

1. **SED/TOI feature is continuous** from midlatitude SED source through dayside cusp and across the polar cap to the midnight auroral region — directly follows convection streamlines.
2. **SED plume >150 TECu** (1 TECu = 10¹⁶ el/m²) extending from Florida/mid-Atlantic states into eastern Canada at the storm's main phase.
3. **F-peak density >1.5×10¹² m⁻³** observed at all three ISR sites as the TOI passed over them; maintained along the full high-latitude trajectory.
4. **Cold plasma signature**: T_e and T_i ≈ 2500 K throughout — consistent with solar-produced midlatitude plasma transported intact, not locally ionized.
5. **F-peak altitude elevated to >500 km** at Millstone Hill as the radar entered the dayside SED (hmF2 rise from ~300 km → >500 km as SED arrived at noon cusp).
6. **O+ upward fluxes >10¹³ m⁻²s⁻¹** observed in the topside ionosphere from the SED/TOI plume within the cusp at Sondrestrom.
7. **Three ISRs sampled different segments** of the TOI life cycle: Millstone Hill (dayside cusp entry at 1730 UT); Sondrestrom (central polar cap at 1830 UT, 1630 MLT); EISCAT Tromso (nightside auroral exit at 1945 UT, near midnight).
8. Each radar observed TOI overhead for ~1 hour as the rotating plume swept under them during the event.
9. SAPS electric field (centered ~45° MLAT at 18 MLT, DMSP data) drives the SED poleward into the cusp.

## Methods/Data

- GPS TEC maps: 2-D coverage of North America from ~450 GPS receivers; 1 TECu precision; resolved SED plume.
- Convection maps: SuperDARN (9 NH radars, 2-min cadence) + DMSP F13 drift meter, fitted with Ruohoniemi & Baker 1998 technique.
- ISRs: Millstone Hill (55°Λ), Sondrestrom (73°Λ), EISCAT Tromso — all in special pre-alert mode for storm.
- DMSP F13: cross-track drift + density along polar pass; SAPS identified at ~45° MLAT, 18 MLT.
- Event: 20 November 2003, 1730–2000 UT (main phase of super-storm).
