# Siscoe 1983 Solar System MHD

*Siscoe, G. L. (1983), Solar System Magnetohydrodynamics, in* Solar-Terrestrial Physics *(eds. R. L. Carovillano and J. M. Forbes), pp. 11–100, D. Reidel.* (The citation is as given in [[Strangeway Ch3 Physics of Magnetized Plasmas]]'s reading list.)

## Summary

A detailed derivation of MHD for space applications. Sections read so far: §II (stagnation points, Eqs. II.62–65), §III (MHD waves, discontinuities and shocks, Eqs. III.55–93) and §IV.1 (firehose and mirror instabilities).

## Key claims

- The firehose instability is the exponential growth of the intermediate (shear) mode when $p_\parallel - p_\perp > B^2/\mu_0$. Physically, the centrifugal force of particles streaming along a bent tube beats the tension (§IV.1).
- The mirror instability is the growth of the slow mode, fastest for $\mathbf{k}$ nearly perpendicular to $\mathbf{B}$. Its threshold is $p_\perp/p_\parallel > 6(1+1/\beta_\perp)$ in CGL-MHD and $p_\perp/p_\parallel > 1+1/\beta_\perp$ in kinetic theory (Eqs. IV.12, IV.14).

- Shock jumps follow from the conservation-law ("continuity") relations. Gas-dynamic shocks compress by at most $(\gamma+1)/(\gamma-1) = 4$ for $\gamma = 5/3$. The pressure jump is unbounded, the downstream flow is subsonic ($M_2^2\to1/5$), and entropy rises only for $M_1 > 1$ (Eqs. III.67–81).
- A parallel shock is identical to a gas-dynamic shock, and the field passes through unchanged. In a perpendicular shock, $B/\rho$ is conserved and the compression solves a quadratic (Eq. III.90). The strong-shock limit is again ×4 (Eq. III.93).
- Discontinuities are classified as contact ($u_n = 0$, $B_n\neq0$), tangential ($u_n = B_n = 0$, total pressure continuous) or shocks.
- At an MHD stagnation point with $\mathbf B\neq0$ the density must vanish (Eq. II.65), unless the flow forms a stagnation line ending in neutral points (Sonnerup 1980).
