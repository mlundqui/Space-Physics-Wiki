# Decadal Survey 2024

**Citation:** National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2024), *The Next Decade of Discovery in Solar and Space Physics: Exploring and Safeguarding Humanity's Home in Space*, National Academies Press, Washington, DC, doi:10.17226/27938. 792 pp.

## Summary

The third decadal survey for solar and space physics (heliophysics), covering 2024–2033. Commissioned by NASA, NSF, NOAA, and AFOSR. Presents a ranked research strategy organized around three science themes, realized through an integrated HelioSystems Laboratory (HSL) framework and a DRIVE+ research program. Strongly endorses GDC + DYNAMIC as the foundational ITM missions (assumed launched as prerequisites for the Appendix D strategy). Roger Varney (UCLA AOS) served as an external reviewer. The ITM panel report (Appendix D, pp. 424–502) is the most relevant section for high-latitude ionosphere research, organized around a system science paradigm with four Priority Science Goals (PSGs), five mission concepts, and five ground-based facility concepts.

## Key claims (survey-level)

- **Science themes:** (1) Sun–Earth–Space: Our Interconnected Home; (2) A Laboratory in Space: Building Blocks of Understanding; (3) New Environments: Exploring Our Cosmic Neighborhood and Beyond.
- **Space weather focus:** Ionospheric HF signal propagation disturbance forecast (1-hr) is an explicit operational priority — connects directly to polar cap patch effects on SuperDARN and communications.
- **Top ITM missions:** GDC + DYNAMIC (2031, assumed prerequisite); new additional concepts BRAVO, Resolve, I-Circuit, LAITIR, SOURCE+.
- **HSL framework:** coordinating structure for missions, ground observatories, community modeling; GDC/DYNAMIC are core.
- **DRIVE+:** continued workforce development, coordination, research tools (CEDAR/GEM/SHINE), and technology development.

## ITM Panel (Appendix D) — Priority Science Goals

### PSG 1: Mass, Momentum, and Energy at Boundaries Across and Within the ITM

- **Obj. 1.1** — Energy/momentum transformation during extreme impulsive events (CMEs, volcanic eruptions, tsunamis, TADs). Hunga Tonga 2022 eruption is a key test case: globally propagating ionospheric disturbances, gravity wave generation, water vapor injection.
- **Obj. 1.2** — Two-way electrodynamic linkages and mass flow between ionosphere, plasmasphere, and magnetosphere. Includes R1 FAC downward electron precipitation → conductivity feedbacks; SAPS/SAIDs; STEVEs; ion upwelling driven by field-aligned heat conduction at R2 latitudes.
- **Obj. 1.3** — ITM response to lower atmosphere forcing on local/global scales. SSW ↔ stratospheric vortex ↔ O/N₂ composition (GOLD observations); DYNAMIC mission fills the 110–250 km "thermospheric gap."
- **Obj. 1.4** — Transition in chemical, dynamical, and thermal drivers across 100–200 km. Critical altitude range where dominant wave modes switch; key knowledge gap.

### PSG 2: Internal ITM Processes Across Spatial and Temporal Scales

- **Obj. 2.1** — Gravity wave spectrum cascades throughout the thermosphere. GWs modulate mean state; adiabatic cooling drives summer mesopause paradox; GWs seed ionospheric TIDs and plasma instabilities. Neither GDC nor DYNAMIC fully observes vertical GW life cycle.
- **Obj. 2.2** — Small-scale high-latitude structuring → mesoscale conductivity enhancements → aggregate global significance. Cross-scale coupling: arc-scale precipitation → meso-conductivity → global FAC distribution. MAGE-class models can capture this.
- **Obj. 2.3** — Nonlinear coupling between mean neutral circulation, tides, and planetary waves. Child waves from nonlinear wave-wave interactions may produce ~50% of primary wave variability at 120 km.
- **Obj. 2.4** — Plasma irregularities driven by and regulating large-scale ITM phenomena. KHI drives SAIDs/STEVEs; Farley-Buneman in auroral E-region; Rayleigh-Taylor seeds equatorial plasma bubbles. Cross-scale coupling mediated by mesoscale conductivity variability is a key knowledge gap.

### PSG 3: Relative Significance of Competing Physical Processes

- **Obj. 3.1** — Preconditioning and seeding mechanisms for ionospheric irregularities. EPBs at low latitudes: multiple competing seeding mechanisms (GWs, TIDs, PPEFs, disturbance dynamo); scintillation affects navigation/communications.
- **Obj. 3.2** — Competing drivers of day-to-day ITM variability. Neutral wind field predictability is the ITM weather equivalent of tropospheric weather; WACCM-X shows orography and tropical cyclones drive ~100 km vertical winds.
- **Obj. 3.3** — Global ITM response during and following geomagnetic activity. Storm-time: neutral wind surges, deep electron density depletions ("super-bubbles"), NO_x polar winter loss; vibrational N₂ enhances O⁺ loss rate → can overestimate F-region e⁻ density by ×2 if not included.
- **Obj. 3.4** — Interhemispheric asymmetries in geospace. Asymmetry in FAC, precipitation, conductivity is now recognized as a common feature; driven by both intrinsic ITM factors (geomagnetic field geometry) and external factors (solar wind, substorm geometry). North magnetic pole migration at >40 km/yr is a slow secular driver.

### PSG 4: ITM Response to Persistent Changes in the Geospace Environment

- **Obj. 4.1** — Slowly evolving natural drivers: Solar Cycle 24 deep minimum (weakest in 100 years); NO and CO₂ emission at 50% and 73% of five-cycle average; IS radars show ion temperature decrease of ~50–100 K/decade.
- **Obj. 4.2** — Anthropogenic changes: CO₂ cooling → thermospheric contraction (−1 to −10% density/decade); methane increase; mega-constellation satellite reentry deposits Al and depletes stratospheric O₃; geoengineering (SO₂ aerosol injection) could alter ITM composition.
- **Obj. 4.3** — Which ITM state parameters are most sensitive to persistent driver changes. Requires decades-long IS radar baselines; solar cycle effects must be detrended to isolate secular trends.
- **Obj. 4.4** — Fundamental physical limitations on ITM dynamic stability. Tipping points in coupled atmosphere system; short observational record limits ability to assess ITM climate shifts.

## ITM Panel — Spaceflight Mission Concepts (D.6.2)

Five notional STP/LWS-class concepts identified (not ranked):

| Mission | Class | Focus |
|---------|-------|-------|
| **BRAVO** | STP | Vertical gravity wave life cycle — 4-satellite constellation at 500 km (NaLiDAR, NIRACs, TLS limb sounders, FUV, GNSS RO); ground meteor radar + GNSS networks |
| **Resolve** | LWS | Global-scale wave spectrum — 72 CubeSats in 12 orbital planes (500–700 km); horizontal neutral wind and temperature profiles; global weather of the 100–200 km region |
| **I-Circuit** | STP | Interhemispheric asymmetry — 2 HEO auroral imagers + 10 LEO in-situ satellites; conjugate auroral images + ionospheric in situ (neutral/ion composition, winds, fields, FACs) |
| **LAITIR** | STP "dipper" | Lower thermosphere below 200 km — 3 sequential satellites, perigee-lowering campaigns to <150 km; Joule heating, ion-neutral coupling, non-Maxwellian regimes |
| **SOURCE+** | STP augment | Ion upwelling/outflow and neutral mass flow — adds 2 satellites to the magnetospheric SOURCE mission to measure ITM boundary at 350–1,500 km; SED–plume–outflow connection |

Key synergies: LAITIR directly measures Joule heating (largest energy input uncertainty); SOURCE+ connects ion upflow to SED plumes directly relevant to [[Zou 2021 Polar Cap Density Structure Advances]]; I-Circuit fills conjugate asymmetry gap.

## ITM Panel — Ground-Based Facility Concepts (D.6.3)

Five ground-based concepts (not ranked):

- **DASHI** (NSF MREFC-class): ≥30 autonomous multi-instrument stations across North + South America, 80–400 km altitude coverage; heterogeneous sensors at each node (FPI, all-sky imager, SDI, SuperDARN, GNSS RX, magnetometer, ionosonde, meteor radar, AMTM). Enables all four PSGs; IS radar sites are ideal DASHI nodes.
- **Meteor Radar Network** (NSF MSRI-2 minimum): MIMO multistatic coverage of continent-scale region for 4D neutral wind fields at 80–100 km; required for BRAVO and Resolve ground truth.
- **Subauroral IS Radar** (NSF MREFC-class): New-generation AMISR-successor in subauroral latitude belt; electronically steerable multi-beam, multistatic, MIMO, broadband — measures SAPS, SAID, SED, SAR arcs, ring current footprints, plasma instabilities; directly addresses PSGs 1.2, 2.1, 2.2, 2.4, 3.1, 3.3. *Explicitly highlighted for ion upflow, plasmasphere coupling, and subauroral phenomena.* See also [[RISR-N]].
- **Extended GNSS Network**: Upgrade geodetic GNSS receivers to science-optimized dual-frequency; expand into low-latitude oceanic and African regions for global TEC and scintillation coverage.
- (Implied) Continued NSF IS radar operations: Jicamarca, Millstone Hill, AMISR systems (PFISR, RISR-N) — long-term secular trend baselines for PSG 4; IS radar facilities are specifically listed as critical DASHI nodes.

## Key contextual findings (D.2)

- **STEVE** recognized as a system-science frontier: supersonic ion flows (~5–10 km/s), Te > 6000 K, fine-scale neutral dynamics — near edge of current modeling capability; connected to SAR arc ring-current footprints.
- **O–O⁺ charge exchange cross section** controversy resolved at Arecibo via optical + IS radar data — removes a major source of F-region model uncertainty; IS radar is uniquely suited for this.
- **Weddell Sea Anomaly** explained primarily by neutral thermospheric composition (O/N₂), not winds — cautions against correlation-based attributions.
- **ICON + GOLD** showed neutral winds at 100–150 km are a dominant driver of ionospheric variability even for weak storms; ITM responds to relatively small disturbances.
- **Antarctic infrastructure gap**: NSF USAP logistics constraints threaten existing SuperDARN, ASIs, magnetometers, and GNSS receivers that are critical for conjugate-hemisphere and polar cap patch studies.

## Methods/data

- Community-driven consensus report: ~450 white papers, five study panels (SHP, MAG, ITM, SWSA, SoP), independent TRACE cost evaluations (Aerospace Corporation).

## Open questions

- Will GDC/DYNAMIC actually launch by 2031 given ongoing budget pressure? Survey assumes both are launched prerequisites.
- Does the Subauroral IS Radar concept displace or complement RISR-N in the long-term NSF portfolio?
- How does the DASHI concept interact with the existing Canadian ASI + magnetometer networks that currently fill the SuperDARN gap in polar coverage?
- What specific PSG-metric success criteria define the 1-hour HF propagation forecast improvement, and how do polar cap patches feed into the forecast chain?
