# Thorne 2010 Chorus Diffuse Aurora

## Summary

A Nature letter (UCLA AOS) settling a long-running debate: are the **diffuse aurora**'s 0.1–30 keV central-plasma-sheet electrons scattered into the loss cone mainly by **electrostatic electron cyclotron harmonic (ECH) waves** or by **whistler-mode chorus**? Using CRRES wave statistics at L = 5 (00–06 MLT), HOTRAY ray-traced wave-normal distributions, bounce-averaged diffusion coefficients and Fokker–Planck simulations: **chorus dominates**. ECH only scatters within about 15° of the loss cone. Chorus scatters the whole injected population within about an hour, reproduces the observed **pancake distributions**, and explains why diffuse aurora is concentrated at 21–06 MLT.

## Key claims

- **Morphology alone can't decide.** The diffuse aurora (PIXIE 2–12 keV X-rays), the plasma-sheet source (CRRES 1.88 keV electrons at L = 4–7, mapping to 60–68° invariant latitude), ECH intensity and chorus intensity all have similar MLT and activity dependence. A quantitative diffusion analysis is needed.
- **The two wave types:**
  - **ECH:** electrostatic, multiple bands between harmonics of $f_{ce}$, propagate nearly perpendicular to $\mathbf{B}$, confined within a few degrees of the equator, driven by loss-cone gradients.
  - **Chorus:** whistler mode, below $f_{ce}$, upper and lower bands with a gap at $f_{ce}/2$, field-aligned propagation over a wide latitude range, driven by injected plasma-sheet electrons.
- **Diffusion rates (L = 5, moderate activity):**
  - **ECH:** fast pitch-angle scattering, but only near the loss cone (below about 15°), for a few hundred eV to a few keV.
  - **Upper-band chorus:** broad pitch-angle scattering for 100 eV to a few keV, reaching into the loss cone below about 3 keV, *and* rapid **momentum (energy) scattering above a few keV**.
  - **Lower-band chorus:** broad pitch-angle scattering for a few keV to 100 keV; most effective for precipitation above about 7 keV.
  - **Combined:** pronounced momentum scattering at 1–10 keV, and loss of the whole 0.1–50 keV injected population in about an hour, shorter than the convection time to the dayside.
- **Fokker–Planck evolution after 1 hour:**
  - ECH alone barely changes the distribution beyond 15°.
  - Upper-band chorus empties low energies over broad pitch angles, giving **pancakes** (peaked at 80–100°) below a few keV, and stochastically accelerates electrons above 10 keV near 90°.
  - Lower-band chorus removes electrons above 10 keV, increasing anisotropy.
  - The combination reproduces CRRES (31 January 1991) and THEMIS dayside distributions.
- **Conclusion:** "Only chorus can explain the remnant pancake electron distribution… and the pronounced depletion of trapped electrons on the dayside." This holds wherever chorus is strong (L below about 8, i.e. invariant latitude below about 70°), regardless of ECH amplitude.

## Methods/data

CRRES plasma wave and LEPA electron data; PIXIE X-ray statistics; HOTRAY ray tracing in a dipole; bounce-averaged quasilinear $\langle D_{\alpha\alpha}\rangle$, $\langle D_{pp}\rangle$, $D_{\alpha p}$; 2-D Fokker–Planck simulation of an injected distribution.
