# Xiong 2020 FACs Precipitation DMSP

*Relationship between large-scale ionospheric field-aligned currents and electron/ion precipitations: DMSP observations.* Xiong, C., Stolle, C., Alken, P., & Rauberg, J. (2020). *Earth, Planets and Space*, 72, 147. doi:10.1186/s40623-020-01286-z.

## Summary

Statistical study of the relationship between field-aligned currents (FACs) derived from DMSP magnetometers and precipitating particle fluxes (electrons and ions) observed simultaneously by DMSP SSIES and SSJ/5 instruments. Investigates Region 1 and Region 2 current geometry and their association with precipitation for southward and northward IMF conditions.

## Key Claims

1. **Both electron and ion precipitation fluxes increase with enhanced southward IMF B_y** — consistent with FAC dependence on B_y.
2. **R2 current maxima at both dusk and dawn sides are co-located with particle energy flux peaks** for southward IMF B_y; this holds in both hemispheres.
3. **R1 currents are displaced ~3.5° equatorward from particle flux peak at the dawn side** — systematic offset between current peak and precipitation peak.
4. **FAC peaks enclose particle precipitation peaks in an auroral band** at both dusk and dawn sides (the statistical mean of FAC peaks brackets the particle peak).
5. **Particle precipitation maxima closer to R1 currents have lower energy fluxes** — opposite to the known feature that R1 is on average stronger than R2 for magnetic field perturbations.
6. The DMSP simultaneous magnetic field + particle measurements allow direct temporal comparison, avoiding the temporal ambiguity of multi-mission cross-comparisons.

## Methods/Data

- DMSP satellites; FACs derived from magnetometer data along-track using the method of Lühr et al. (2020) — assumes elongated E-W current sheets.
- Particle data from SSIES (thermal plasma) and SSJ/5 (precipitation spectrometer).
- Statistical analysis; data sorted by IMF B_y and B_z components.

## Open Questions

- Whether the 3.5° displacement between R1 and precipitation is a time-aliasing artifact (current and precipitation measured sequentially) or a real spatial offset.
- How the FAC–precipitation geometry changes during substorm onset vs quiet time.
