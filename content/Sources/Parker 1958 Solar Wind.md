# Parker 1958 Solar Wind

**Citation:** Parker, E. N. (1958), *Dynamics of the Interplanetary Gas and Magnetic Fields*, Astrophys. J., 128, 664–676.

## Summary

Foundational paper predicting the continuous supersonic outflow of the hot solar corona — the solar wind. Parker demonstrates that the static barometric equilibrium equation applied to a fully ionized 10⁶ K corona yields a pressure at infinity that is finite and much larger than the interstellar medium pressure; static equilibrium is therefore thermodynamically impossible and a continuous transonic outflow is required. The outstreaming plasma carries the coronal magnetic field, drawing it into an Archimedean spiral (Parker spiral). The 500–1500 km/s speeds and ~500 ions/cm³ density Parker used were Biermann's comet-tail inferences, not predictions of the model. Mariner 2 (1962) confirmed a continuous supersonic outflow of several hundred km/s, but at a density of about 5–10 cm⁻³. *(Corrected 2026-10-08: this line previously said the density was confirmed.)* The polar wind is the direct terrestrial analogue: the same Parker transonic solution applied to open polar cap field lines, driven by the ambipolar electric potential.

## Key claims

- Static coronal equilibrium with thermal conductivity κ(T) ∝ T^(5/2): the steady-state temperature profile T(r) ∝ r^(−2/7) yields P(∞) > 0; since P_ISM ≈ 0, static equilibrium is impossible.
- Transonic Parker solution: sonic point at r_c = GM_⊙m/4k_BT_0 ≈ 5–10 R_⊙; flow is subsonic below and supersonic above the sonic point; asymptotic velocity ~500 km/s at 1 AU.
- Solar mass loss: ~10¹⁴ g/s, consistent with Biermann comet-tail observations.
- Frozen-in magnetic field (ideal MHD): outstreaming plasma draws the solar dipole field radially outward; solar rotation then winds the field into an Archimedean spiral B_r ∝ r⁻², B_φ ∝ r⁻¹ at large r (the Parker spiral).
- Density ~500 ions/cm³ at Earth orbit was taken from Biermann's comet-tail analysis as an input (used to get the 10¹⁴ g/s mass loss); the measured value is about 5–10 cm⁻³. The spiral geometry ($B_\phi/B_r\sim\Omega r/V$) was confirmed. *(Corrected 2026-10-08.)* Derivation: [[Parker Solar Wind and Spiral]].

## Methods/data

- Purely theoretical: spherically symmetric, steady-state, hydrodynamic equations for a fully ionized hydrogen corona; thermal conductivity κ ≈ 5×10⁻⁷ T^(5/2) erg cm⁻¹ s⁻¹ K⁻¹. No observational data.

## Connections

- [[Polar Wind]] — Parker's transonic solution is the parent of the polar wind concept (Banks & Holzer 1968 applied the identical formalism to open polar field lines, with ambipolar electric potential replacing coronal pressure as the driving force).
- [[High-Speed Streams]] — fast solar wind from coronal holes is the Parker solution with higher boundary temperature/velocity; CH/HSS-driven geomagnetic activity connects back to the Parker wind structure.
- [[Atmospheric Escape]] — Parker wind is the planetary analogue for hydrodynamic escape from early atmospheric histories; the same pressure-gradient-driven transonic outflow.
- [[Dungey Cycle]] — solar wind interaction with the magnetosphere drives reconnection and the two-cell convection pattern (Dungey 1961).

## Open questions

- What heats the corona to 10⁶ K? (Parker's own 1988 nanoflare hypothesis is a leading candidate; still unresolved.)
- How does the solar wind interact with a planetary magnetosphere to drive geomagnetic storms? (see [[Dungey Cycle]])
