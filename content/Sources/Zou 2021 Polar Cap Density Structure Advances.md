# Zou 2021 Polar Cap Density Structure Advances

*Recent Advances in Polar Cap Density Structure Research.* Zou, S., Perry, G.W., & Foster, J.C. (2021). In C. Huang & G. Lu (Eds.), *Ionosphere Dynamics and Applications*, Geophysical Monograph 260, Ch. 4, pp. 67–82. DOI:10.1002/9781119815617.ch4.

## Summary

Comprehensive review of post-2012 progress on polar cap high-density ionospheric structures, covering statistical occurrence rates, internal plasma characteristics from AMISR imaging, dynamic horizontal and vertical transport, ion upflow mechanisms, and optical emission variability. Builds directly on Carlson (2012) and Crowley (1996) reviews; reviews advances enabled by RISR-C/N and expanded GPS networks.

## Key Claims

**Occurrence statistics:**
- Patches occur more frequently in **December in both hemispheres** (not local winter), as confirmed by Chartier et al. 2018 with F_10.7-linked threshold (see [[Chartier 2017 Swarm Patch Occurrence]]).
- Occurrence shows no clear linear relationship with Kp — relationship with geomagnetic activity is complicated by storm-phase composition effects and TOI/patch dependence on interplay between convection and thermospheric winds.
- IMF B_y controls patch location within the cusp; southward B_z dominates patch formation rate.

**Internal plasma characteristics (from RISR-C, Ren et al. 2018 automated study of 400+ patches):**
- Median patch NmF2 decreases by ~42% from dayside (~3.6×10¹¹ m⁻³) to nightside (~2.1×10¹¹ m⁻³).
- Patch median T_e ~380 K **lower** than sector background (consistent with cold SED-origin plasma).
- Median ion fluxes within patches are **downward** in all sectors except noon.
- Bottom F-region density inside patches lower than surroundings at noon → SED lifting signature.

**Hot patches:**
- "Hot" patches (elevated T_e, identified at DMSP ~850 km by Zhang et al. 2017) associated with particle precipitation and convection flow shear; may co-occur with classically-formed cold patches or be independently produced.

**Dynamic evolution:**
- Patches convect at ExB velocity; can be tracked in GPS TEC maps.
- Zhang et al. 2015: full Dungey cycle ~3 hours; patches convect from dayside entry to nightside exit.
- Hosokawa et al. 2010: patch bifurcation when convection field diverges.

**Optical emissions:**
- 630 nm redline emission: recombination-dominated (Link & Cogger 1988 formula); but Perry et al. 2013 showed luminosity can change factor of 2 with enhanced ExB convection (descending F region), and Zou et al. 2017 found soft precipitation contributes even to non-storm-time patches.
- Perry & St.-Maurice 2018: higher-altitude patches emit dimmer 630 nm; can be missed by imagers.
- Sojka factor-of-4 altitude ambiguity documented.

**Ion upflow:**
- Type 1 (frictional heating): ~100–750 m/s below 1000 km, associated with convecting patches entering particle precipitation regions.
- Type 2 (ambipolar + FAC): divergent fluxes observed when patches cross nightside auroral boundary.
- Peak observed flux ~2×10¹⁴ m⁻²s⁻¹ from Zou et al. 2017b when SED plume crossed open-closed boundary at PFISR.
- Yuan et al. 2008: DMSP measured O+ vertical flux ~1.2×10¹⁴ m⁻²s⁻¹ within SED at nightside polar boundary.

## Open Questions

- Quantitative separation of classical cold patches vs hot patches by mechanism (precipitation vs convection shear).
- Ion-neutral interaction inside patches requires fully coupled IT models; MSIS neutral background may not capture patch-scale effects.
- Best definition of a patch remains instrument-dependent (relative doubling vs absolute density; bottomside vs topside).
