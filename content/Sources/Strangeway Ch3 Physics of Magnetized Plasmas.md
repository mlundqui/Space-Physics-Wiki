# Strangeway Ch3 Physics of Magnetized Plasmas

> **Provenance caveat:** The PDF metadata names Bob Strangeway as author, and the file was created in February 2016. As with [[Strangeway Ch11 The Aurora]], this is presumably a chapter of Russell, Luhmann & Strangeway, *Space Physics: An Introduction* (Cambridge, 2016), used as the EPSS 200C text. That is **inferred, not confirmed**. Equation numbering in the PDF is occasionally inconsistent (e.g. "[3.3.10]" for [3.17]).

## Summary

A 57-page chapter that builds the two main descriptions of a magnetized plasma from scratch and then shows they agree. Particle orbit theory covers gyration, the $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}$, $\nabla B$, curvature and polarization drifts, a generalized drift equation, and the three adiabatic invariants. Fluid theory runs from the Boltzmann/Vlasov equation through moments to single-fluid MHD, the generalized Ohm's law and the frozen-in theorem. It closes with MHD waves (using a two-triad geometric construction), the equivalence of drift and MHD currents, and the Vasyliunas-type expression for field-aligned currents.

## Key claims

- The force drift $\mathbf{v}_F = \mathbf{F}\times\mathbf{B}/qB^2$ generates $\nabla B$ and curvature drifts, given $\langle\mathbf{F}\rangle = -\mu\nabla B$ and the centrifugal force (Eqs. 3.18, 3.36, 3.39). The curvature drift is re-derived without fictitious forces by Taylor expansion (§3.3.5).
- $\mu$ is invariant for both time-varying and spatially varying fields (Eqs. 3.19–3.30). The three invariants are listed in §3.3.7.
- Drift current plus magnetization current equals the MHD perpendicular current; the $\nabla B$-drift current cancels against part of the magnetization current (§3.7.2, after Parker 1957 and Northrop 1963).
- The MHD energy equation closes with $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{q} = 0$, not $\mathbf{q}=0$ (§3.5.3).
- The quasi-neutral electric force is of order $u^2/c^2$ relative to $\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{B}$ (Eq. 3.139).
- "The field is frozen to the fluid," not the reverse. Moving field lines are a construct. Corotation is imposed by currents, not by equipotential mapping (§3.6.4).
- The shear (intermediate) mode alone carries field-aligned current; the bow shock is a standing fast-mode wave (§3.7.1, §3.7.4).
- Field-aligned currents arise from the pressure term and inertia (flow braking, vorticity) (Eq. 3.204).
