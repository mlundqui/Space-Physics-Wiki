# Pierrard 2001 Solar Wind Electrons

**Citation:** Pierrard, V., M. Maksimovic, and J. Lemaire (2001), *Core, Halo and Strahl Electrons in the Solar Wind*, Astrophys. Space Sci., 277, 195–200.

## Summary

Kinetic Fokker-Planck model for solar wind electron velocity distribution functions (VDFs) from the collisional corona to the collisionless interplanetary medium at 1 AU. The electron VDF consists of three components: (1) a thermal core — cool electrons collisionally trapped within the ambipolar thermo-electric potential well Φ_E of the heliosphere; (2) an isotropic suprathermal halo present at all pitch angles; (3) a field-aligned strahl observed primarily in the fast solar wind. The model solves the kinetic equation including Coulomb collisions from the corona (collision-dominated) to 1 AU (nearly collisionless). Key result: suprathermal tails are present in the corona when a strahl is observed at large distances, even at low altitudes where Coulomb collisions are important.

## Key claims

- Three-component solar wind electron VDF at 1 AU: thermal core (trapped below Φ_E), isotropic halo (suprathermal, present at all pitch angles), field-aligned strahl (present primarily in fast wind).
- Core electrons are collisionally trapped within the ambipolar potential well; halo and strahl have energies sufficient to escape (E > eΦ_E).
- Strahl arises from conservation of the magnetic moment during outward propagation: electrons focused field-aligned by the decreasing B; anisotropy increases with solar distance in a collisionless model.
- Halo isotropy is not explained by collisionless models (which predict only an anti-sunward strahl): wave-particle scattering, large-scale magnetic field structures, or CIR reflections are needed.
- Fokker-Planck (Coulomb collision operator): suprathermal tails arise at low coronal altitudes when a strahl is observed at large distances — the suprathermal population is set up in the corona, not generated en route.

## Methods/data

- Numerical solution of the kinetic Boltzmann equation with Fokker-Planck Coulomb collision operator; external forces: gravitational (negligible for electrons), ambipolar electric, Lorentz.
- Boundary conditions: Maxwellian at the coronal base; comparison with Helios/Wind in-situ electron VDF observations at 1 AU.

## Connections

- [[Wave-Particle Interactions]] — wave scattering (whistler-mode, EMIC) is proposed as the mechanism isotropizing the strahl into the observed halo; strahl pitch-angle width carries information about wave environments between Sun and Earth.
- [[Polar Wind]] — the ambipolar electric potential Φ_E driving strahl electron escape is mathematically identical to the ambipolar potential driving H⁺ polar wind outflow; both require going beyond fluid models to capture the transonic/suprathermal population correctly.
- [[Plasma Waves]] — strahl-driven whistler waves are a proposed strahl-scattering mechanism; heat flux whistlers are generated at the strahl speed.

## Open questions

- What physical process isotropizes the strahl into the halo? (Wave scattering, CIR reflections, or other?)
- How does the strahl/halo partitioning depend on solar wind speed, solar distance, and solar cycle?
