# Tsurutani 2013 PPEF Ionosphere Comment

*Comment on "Storming the Bastille: the effect of electric fields on the ionospheric F-layer" by Rishbeth et al. (2010).* Tsurutani, B. T., et al. (2013). *Annales Geophysicae*, 31, 145–150. doi:10.5194/angeo-31-145-2013.

*Note: PDF filename incorrectly labeled 2021; paper was published 2013.*

## Summary

Short comment/rebuttal (6 pp, JPL) arguing that large TEC enhancements at middle latitudes (up to ~270–300 TECu, EIA peaks displaced poleward to ±22–30° MLAT) during the 30 October 2003 Halloween superstorm are explained by **prompt penetrating electric fields (PPEFs)** driving anomalous E×B upward drift and the "superfountain effect," not by plasma transport from equatorial latitudes. SAMI2 model calculations with and without the PPEF confirm that the poleward displacement and enhanced magnitude of the equatorial ionization anomaly (EIA) peaks are reproduced by the PPEF mechanism. Disturbance winds from auroral Joule heating may also contribute but cannot be resolved from spacecraft data alone.

## Key Claims

1. **PPEFs of ~4 mV/m** during the 30 October 2003 storm drive strong E×B vertical drift of equatorial ionospheric plasma (superfountain effect), displacing EIA peaks from their normal ±10° MLAT location to ±22–30° MLAT.
2. **TEC at displaced EIA peaks reaches ~210–270 TECu** in CHAMP data — values that cannot be explained by equatorial plasma transport to mid-latitudes within the chemical/dynamical lifetime of the plasma.
3. **SAMI2 model** with PPEF reproduces both the poleward displacement and the enhanced intensity of the EIA peaks; without PPEF, peaks remain near ±10° MLAT.
4. **Rishbeth et al. (2010) are incorrect** to imply that transport from the equatorial source can account for the high TEC at middle and high magnetic latitudes — the plasma cannot travel far enough in its lifetime without the PPEF mechanism.
5. Post-storm TEC enhancements at ±30° MLAT persist 2 hr after PPEF ends due to slow recombination at elevated altitudes.

## Methods/Data

- CHAMP satellite TEC at ~400 km altitude during the 30 October 2003 magnetic storm.
- SAMI2 low-latitude ionospheric model (Huba et al. 2000, 2002) with PPEF specified from Kyoto conductivity model and Rostogi-Klobuchar technique.
- Comparison of CHAMP passes at 12:30–13:30 MLT before, during, and after the storm.

## Open Questions

- Quantitative partition between PPEF superfountain and disturbance wind effects during the storm main phase — CHAMP data alone cannot separate them.
- Whether SAMI2's simplified equatorial representation captures the full storm-time plasma redistribution.
