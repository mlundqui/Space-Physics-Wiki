# Keiling 2003 Alfvén Wave Poynting Flux

> The PDF begins with the last page of an unrelated Science report on patterned ground; Keiling et al. starts partway through.

## Summary

One year (1997, about 470 orbits) of Polar electric and magnetic field data at **25,000–38,000 km altitude**. The field-aligned **Alfvén-wave Poynting flux** (6–180 s periods, which excludes quasi-static FACs) **traces the statistical auroral oval**. It matches Polar UVI auroral luminosity in shape, with peaks at 21–24 and about 15 MLT. It is overwhelmingly **downward** with very little returning, so it is dissipated below the spacecraft, presumably in the auroral acceleration region. **It can supply about 30–35% of the energy behind global auroral luminosity.** Individual events above 5 erg cm⁻² s⁻¹ (mapped to 100 km) occur all around the oval.

## Key claims

- **Morphology.** Wave Poynting flux mapped to 100 km delineates the oval, with two maxima (21:00–00:00 and about 15:00 MLT) matching the UVI LBH-long luminosity peaks (22:30, 15:00, 09:00).
- **Direction.** Upward Poynting flux is very small, so there is no significant ionospheric reflection back to Polar altitudes. The energy is dissipated below, presumably in the acceleration region at **5000–15,000 km**.
- **Energy budget.** The wave Poynting flux can account for **about 30–35%** of the energy flux needed for global auroral luminosity. This is an estimate: different time periods, and activity varies.
- **Sufficiency.** The 5 erg cm⁻² s⁻¹ threshold is chosen because about 1 erg cm⁻² s⁻¹ is the weakest visible aurora. Many events exceed 30 erg cm⁻² s⁻¹. Long traces of large events appear during storms (10 January 1997, 3 August 1997).
- **Change in view.** Large-scale FACs are **not the only** electromagnetic energy carrier powering auroral acceleration. Alfvén waves couple the magnetosphere and ionosphere over the *entire* oval. In one event, wave Poynting flux was 1–2 orders of magnitude larger than FAC-associated Poynting flux.

## Methods/data

Polar EFI and MFE fields. $S_\parallel = (\delta\mathbf{E}\times\delta\mathbf{B}/\mu_0)\cdot\hat{\mathbf{B}}$, band-passed to 6–180 s and averaged over 30 s to suppress standing waves. Mapped to 100 km assuming dissipationless propagation along converging field lines. Binned 2° MLAT × 0.75 h MLT. Compared with UVI (17,372 images, April–July 1997).
