# Treumann 2002 Auroral Electrodynamics

*Auroral Plasma Physics.* Treumann, R. A., et al. (2002). *Space Science Reviews*, 103, 1–485. (Chapter 6: Electrodynamics of Auroral Forms, pp. 251–350.)

## Summary

Comprehensive review volume on auroral plasma physics (32 authors, Max Planck Institute and collaborators). Chapter 6, "Electrodynamics of Auroral Forms," covers the ionospheric electrodynamics of discrete auroral arcs, substorm auroral bulge features (Westward Traveling Surge, Omega Bands, Auroral Streamers), and polar cap aurora. Addresses how field-aligned currents (FACs) close through ionospheric Pedersen and Hall conductance, the electric field patterns associated with each auroral form type, and their relationship to magnetospheric drivers. Integrates ground-based, low-altitude satellite, rocket, and numerical modeling results.

## Key Claims

1. **Discrete auroral arc electrodynamics** can be described as a quasi-static current circuit: the arc's enhanced conductivity channels Pedersen current across the arc while Hall current produces the well-known electrojets; the arc-associated electric field pattern depends on the ratio of field-aligned to Pedersen conductance.
2. **Westward Traveling Surge (WTS)** is the optical signature of the westward-propagating substorm expansion onset; its electrodynamics are driven by divergence of field-aligned currents at the leading edge.
3. **Omega Bands** appear at the poleward boundary of the diffuse aurora during substorm recovery; their electrodynamics involve periodic FAC sheets associated with the magnetospheric Kelvin-Helmholtz instability.
4. **Auroral Streamers** are north-south-aligned forms at the poleward auroral boundary, believed to be the ionospheric signature of bursty bulk flows (BBFs) in the central plasma sheet.
5. **Polar cap aurora** during northward IMF (theta aurora, transpolar arcs) is covered in Section 6.5; associated with lobe convection and cusp-to-nightside reconnection.

## Methods/Data

Review chapter. Ground-based all-sky imagers, low-altitude satellites (Viking, FAST), sounding rockets, SuperDARN; numerical MHD + current-continuity models.

## Open Questions

- Dynamical (time-varying) coupling between FAC closure, arc development, and magnetospheric reconnection — Chapter 8 of the same volume covers this.
- Quantitative treatment of arc fine-structure (narrow acceleration channels < 1 km) remains limited by in-situ spatial resolution.
