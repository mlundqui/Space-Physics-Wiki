# Gallardo-Lacourt 2018 STEVE Statistics

*A Statistical Analysis of STEVE.* Gallardo-Lacourt, B., et al. (2018). *Journal of Geophysical Research: Space Physics*, 123, 9893–9905. doi:10.1029/2018JA025368.

## Summary

First statistical study of STEVE (Strong Thermal Emission Velocity Enhancement), an optical sub-auroral phenomenon documented by citizen scientists and recently identified as scientifically distinct from conventional aurora. Characterizes 28 STEVE events identified via THEMIS all-sky imager (ASI) network and REGO red-line camera database. STEVE appears as a latitudinally narrow (~20 km), zonally extended (~2145 km) mauve/purple emission band equatorward of the auroral oval, observed for ~1 hr. All 28 events occurred 1 hr after substorm onset, at the end of a prolonged substorm expansion phase. STEVE is not caused by conventional particle precipitation.

## Key Claims

1. **STEVE is latitudinally very narrow (~20 km N-S)** but longitudinally extensive (>2000 km), located sub-aurorally in the ~63–64° MLAT range, equatorward of the auroral oval boundary.
2. **All 28 events occurred ~1 hr after substorm onset**, at the end of a prolonged (~1 hr) expansion phase — STEVE is strongly substorm-linked but appears after, not during, peak activity.
3. **AL index magnitude is larger and expansion phase longer** for STEVE events than typical substorms — STEVE requires particularly intense substorm activity.
4. **Equatorward displacement ~50 km** from initial detection location over STEVE's ~1 hr lifetime.
5. **Not conventional aurora**: prior work (Archer et al. 2019) showed absence of particle precipitation at STEVE's location — the optical emission cannot be explained by energetic particle impact ionization; a different (thermal?) emission mechanism is required.
6. **Connection to subauroral ion drifts (SAID/SAPS)**: the substorm-linked timing and sub-auroral location are consistent with STEVE being associated with the intense narrow SAPS velocity channel.

## Methods/Data

- THEMIS ASI network: 21 events from Dec 2007–Dec 2015.
- REGO database (red-line optical): 7 events from Nov 2014–Dec 2017; 3-s cadence.
- Keograms (north-south slices) used to characterize latitudinal motion and duration.
- OMNI solar wind data and SuperMAG AL index for geomagnetic context.

## Open Questions

- Physical emission mechanism: if not particle impact, is it thermal emission from the hot SAPS ion population, or another process?
- Whether STEVE is primarily an ionospheric or thermospheric phenomenon.
- Geographic/seasonal occurrence statistics — this study is limited to the North American sector covered by THEMIS ASIs.
