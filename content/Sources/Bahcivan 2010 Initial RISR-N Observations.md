# Bahcivan 2010 Initial RISR-N Observations

*Initial ionospheric observations made by the new Resolute incoherent scatter radar and comparison to solar wind IMF.* Bahcivan, H., Tsunoda, R., Nicolls, M., & Heinselman, C. (2010). *Geophysical Research Letters*, 37, L15103. doi:10.1029/2010GL043632.

## Summary

First published scientific results from RISR-N at Resolute Bay (completed 2009). Presents 8 days of F-region observations in September 2009 near equinox, demonstrating that polar cap N_e structures are tightly controlled by IMF B_z southward turnings and confirming solar-produced (not particle-precipitation) plasma as the primary source. Validates the Lockwood & Carlson 1992 transient-reconnection patch hypothesis.

## Key Claims

1. **N_e enhancements tied to strong (≥5 nT) IMF B_z southward turnings**; appear at the bow shock and are shifted ~30–40 min to the ionosphere.
2. **Arrival delays 25–75 min depending on MLT**: ~40 min at noon MLT sector (cusp closer); ~80–90 min at midnight sector (patch must travel ~2400 km at ~500 m/s antisunward).
3. **Duration matches B_z southward interval**: enhancements last 0.5–2 h, then N_e returns to background when B_z turns northward.
4. **Horizontal substructures ~25 km in ExB direction** are embedded within larger-scale patches (~150 km elongated perpendicular to motion).
5. **Altitude profiles show NO localization** (uniform altitude distribution) → characteristic of solar EUV-produced plasma, not mono-energetic particle precipitation.
6. **Anti-sunward flow** (convection intensification) is the most predictable response to strong B_z southward; full convection is not simply 2-cell or 4-cell in detail.
7. **Supports Lockwood & Carlson 1992**: quasi-continuous transient magnetopause reconnection every 1–2 min brings solar-produced plasma across the cusp.
8. Confirms Carlson et al. 2006: co-rotating sub-auroral plasma structures injected directly into the polar cap at km/s speeds.

## Methods/Data

- 11 radar beams, 441.9 MHz, 1.8 MW peak power, 10% duty cycle, alt range 0–743 km, 75-s time resolution.
- RISR geographic lat 74.73°N, lon −94.91°E; magnetic lat 82.77°N; MLT ≈ UT−7 hrs; solar local time ≈ UT−6 hrs.
- WIND spacecraft IMF data time-shifted to bow shock (solar wind speed ~360 km/s during event); additional 30-min visual delay applied.
- 10 IMF B_z southward turning events analyzed; flow intensifications average 23 min before N_e enhancements.
