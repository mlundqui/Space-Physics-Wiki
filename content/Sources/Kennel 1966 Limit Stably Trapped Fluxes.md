# Kennel 1966 Limit Stably Trapped Fluxes

*Limit on Stably Trapped Particle Fluxes.* Kennel, C.F. & Petschek, H.E. (1966). *Journal of Geophysical Research*, 71(1), 1–28.

## Summary

Foundational paper establishing the **Kennel-Petschek limit**: the maximum stable flux of trapped particles in the radiation belts, set by a self-regulating wave-particle interaction. When trapped particles become sufficiently anisotropic (T_⊥ > T_∥), they drive whistler-mode or ion cyclotron waves whose pitch-angle diffusion precipitates particles until the distribution relaxes to marginal stability. The paper derives the strong/weak diffusion regimes and predicts the limiting trapped fluxes.

## Key Claims

1. **Self-regulation**: whistler-mode wave growth → pitch-angle diffusion → particle precipitation → wave energy depleted; growth rate adjusts to balance precipitation loss rate. System evolves toward marginal stability.
2. **Strong diffusion limit** ($D_{\alpha\alpha} > D_c$): the loss cone is full and the *precipitation rate* is at its maximum. The minimum lifetime is $T_L^*\approx2T_B/\alpha_0^2\approx4T_BL^3$ (Eq. 4.13). *(Corrected 2026-10-07: this previously said "Flux cannot exceed this level." Strong diffusion caps the loss rate, not the trapped flux. The stably-trapped-flux limit $J^*$ applies to weak diffusion, and a strong enough source can push trapped flux beyond it; Summers et al. 2009 §1. See [[Kennel-Petschek Limit]].)*
3. **Weak diffusion limit** (D_αα < D_c): loss cone is nearly empty; precipitation rate ∝ D_αα; wave amplitude must adjust to maintain marginal stability.
4. **Limiting flux** (Eqs. 5.1–5.4): wave growth balances wave escape, $\gamma\approx(\ln G)/T_w$ with $\ln G\approx3$. For $>40$ keV electrons this gives $J^*\approx7\times10^{10}/L^4$ cm$^{-2}$ s$^{-1}$ at dawn and evening, accurate to about a factor of 3.
5. **Observational test** (§5.2, Fig. 3): the highest Explorer 14 equatorial fluxes of >40 keV electrons lie close to $J^*$ at each $L$. For $L < 4$ the fluxes were "always well below critical" with little precipitation. For $L > 4$ they were "often critical," with considerable precipitation (Injun 3). *(Corrected 2026-10-07: the $L$-dependence was previously stated backwards. The proton >120 keV comparison is in a later section not re-checked here.)*
6. **Limiting proton and electron fluxes are roughly equal**, partially explaining why the outer belt is dominated by electrons and inner belt by protons.
7. The required equatorial whistler-mode wave background intensity (~10⁻⁴ γ) is consistent with observations and particle lifetimes.
8. Pitch-angle diffusion coefficient for weak scattering: D_αα ∝ (b/B₀)² × Ω (wave magnetic fluctuation amplitude squared × cyclotron frequency).

## Methods/Data

Linear wave growth theory (whistler and ion cyclotron modes); Fokker-Planck equation for pitch-angle diffusion; steady-state solution for trapped particle distribution in a finite magnetosphere. Observational data from Explorers 14 and 12 for electron and proton fluxes at various L shells.
