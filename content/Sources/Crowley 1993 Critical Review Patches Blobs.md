# Crowley 1993 Critical Review Patches Blobs

*Critical Review of Ionospheric Patches and Blobs.* Crowley, G. In W.R. Stone (Ed.), *Review of Radio Science 1993–1996*, pp. 619–648. New York: Oxford University Press, 1996.

## Summary

Foundational review of the morphology, definitions, and formation mechanisms of ionospheric patches (F-region density enhancements inside the polar cap) and blobs (outside the polar cap at auroral/sub-auroral latitudes). Reviews work through 1993 and introduces the operational definitions that became field standards. Written before RISR existed; ISR data come from Chatanika, Sondrestrom, and EISCAT.

## Key Claims

- **Patch definition**: plasma-density enhancements (by a factor of ≥2 above background) that have entered, or are about to enter, the polar cap; scale ≥100 km horizontal; optical alternative: ≥50 R above 6300 Å background or ≥50% above background brightness (agreed at 1994 Peaceful Valley workshop).
- **Blob definition**: plasma enhancements that have already passed through the polar cap and are now in the nightside auroral/sub-auroral region; three subtypes in order of increasing latitude: sub-auroral blob (in the ionospheric trough), boundary blob (at poleward edge of trough), auroral blob (within the auroral oval, likely precipitation-produced).
- **Source plasma**: dayside solar EUV-produced plasma is the dominant source; Buchau et al. 1985 showed patch densities match sub-cusp foF2, not locally produced values.
- **Entry mechanism**: plasma transported through dayside cusp/throat under the high-latitude convection pattern; smooth TOI fragmented into discrete patches by IMF Bz variations (southward → patches; northward → sun-aligned arcs).
- **Patchiness models**: Anderson et al. 1988 showed larger convection-pattern radius → denser polar cap; rapidly changing convection (bursty reconnection, Lockwood & Carlson 1992) → "spiky" patches; slow change → "broad" patches.
- **Sub-auroral blobs** form when auroral oval retreats poleward during substorm recovery, stranding boundary-blob plasma at lower latitudes ("fossil" F-region trough mechanism, Evans et al. 1983).
- **Auroral blobs** extend only to low altitudes (<200 km) → precipitation origin; short temporal persistence.
- RISR at Resolute Bay was planned but not yet operational (1993); Crowley anticipates it will define patch morphology.

## Methods/Data

Review article. Draws on published work (1963–1993): DE-2, Chatanika ISR, Sondrestrom ISR, EISCAT, Millstone Hill ISR, all-sky 6300 Å imagers, digital ionosondes, SuperDARN (then nascent), DMSP. Figure 1 reproduces Weber et al. 1984 all-sky image sequence of antisunward patch drift.

## Open Questions

- Exact mechanism connecting IMF Bz transitions to TOI fragmentation not resolved (1993).
- Whether boundary blobs are simply reconfigured patches or have additional particle-precipitation input.
- UT/seasonal control of patch occurrence rate (partially explained by Buchau et al. 1985 sub-cusp latitude geometry).
