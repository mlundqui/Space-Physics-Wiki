# Diaz Pena 2021 Auroral Heating Patches

*Auroral Heating of Plasma Patches Due to High-Latitude Reconnection.* Díaz Peña, J., et al. (2021). *Journal of Geophysical Research: Space Physics*, 126, e2021JA029657. doi:10.1029/2021JA029657.

## Summary

RISR-N case study of 24 January 2012 showing how a cold polar cap patch co-mingled with a soft-electron precipitation region (a transpolar arc produced by high-latitude lobe reconnection), producing a region of elevated electron density and temperature — a "hot patch" signature at DMSP altitudes. GEMINI model simulations demonstrate that the hot patch is produced by auroral heating and upward diffusion, not direct particle impact ionization. Varney is a co-author.

## Key Claims

1. **Solar wind context**: IMF B_x > 0, B_y < 0 throughout — conditions favorable for high-latitude (lobe) reconnection; sharp B_z southward transient at ~11:15 UT within a period of northward B_z.
2. **RISR-N observation**: a cold F-region plasma patch (typical solar-produced density) convected into an intensifying sun-aligned arc (luminous transpolar arc associated with lobe reconnection flux tube).
3. **Result of co-mingling**: contemporaneous increase in T_e above 200 km and N_e below 250 km while leaving N_e near the F-region peak largely unchanged.
4. **DMSP inference**: the DMSP satellites at ~833 km observe elevated N_e + T_e — this is the signature of a "hot patch" in classic DMSP studies (Zhang et al. 2017).
5. **Key finding**: **hot patch produced by auroral heating + upward diffusion, not by particle impact ionization** at F-region altitudes. GEMINI model confirms: correlated enhancement in N_e and T_e at DMSP altitudes arises from diffusion of plasma lifted by precipitation-driven heating, not from in-situ ionization increase at the F peak.
6. **Novel RISR-N technique**: pulse-by-pulse beam steering used to construct time-dependent volumetric images allowing plasma to be tracked in the moving reference frame of the drifting patch.
7. Lobe reconnection generates soft electron precipitation (poleward-moving auroral forms / PMAFs) as magnetic stress is released; the resulting precipitation heats electrons without necessarily adding ionization at F-region altitudes.

## Methods/Data

- RISR-N at 74.73°N, 94.91°W; 42-beam pattern; 2-min integration; volumetric density/T_e/T_i/V_LOS images.
- OMTI all-sky imager at Resolute Bay: 630 nm (2-min cadence, 30-s exposure) for transpolar arc imaging.
- DMSP F15–F18 thermal plasma analysis (SSIES) and precipitation spectrometer (SSJ/5); focus on F16 (closest pass).
- OMNI solar wind IMF data, time-shifted to bow shock.
- GEMINI (Zettergren & Semeter 2012): fluid model (3-moment, mass/momentum/energy) for plasma; self-consistently coupled to neutral atmosphere; run at high spatial resolution around the Resolute Bay region.

## Open Questions

- Whether this heating+diffusion mechanism can explain a substantial fraction of all hot patches observed statistically by DMSP, or is specific to the lobe-reconnection condition.
- Relationship between cold patch evolution and subsequent heating: does a cold patch always become a hot patch when entering precipitation regions, or does it depend on patch age/density?
