# Kelley Earth's Ionosphere

*The Earth's Ionosphere: Plasma Physics and Electrodynamics*, 2nd ed., Michael C. Kelley (Cornell). Academic Press.

## Summary

The standard graduate text on ionospheric electrodynamics and plasma instabilities. So far only Ch. 2 §2.2 (steady-state plasma motions, mobility and conductivity tensors) has been read, for [[Pedersen and Hall Conductivity]].

## Key claims (Ch. 2 §2.2)

- Conductivity tensor with specific (parallel), Pedersen and Hall components, written in terms of $\kappa_j = q_jB/M_j\nu_{jn}$ (Eqs. 2.38–2.39).
- $|\kappa_e| = 1$ near 75 km and $\kappa_i = 1$ near 130 km, for the equatorial ionosphere with $B = 0.25$ G and $\nu_e = \nu_{en}+\nu_{ei}$ (Fig. 2.5).
- For $\kappa_i\gg1$ (above about 130 km), $\sigma_P = nM\nu_{in}/B^2$ (Eq. 2.40b).
- $\sigma_H$ matters only where $\kappa_e\gg1$, $\kappa_i<1$ and $n$ is large; $\sigma_0/\sigma_P > 10^4$ above 130 km.

## Methods/data

Analytical, with model profiles of typical daytime midlatitude conductivities (Fig. 2.6).
