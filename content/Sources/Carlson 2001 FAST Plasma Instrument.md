# Carlson 2001 FAST Plasma Instrument

*The Electron and Ion Plasma Experiment for FAST.* Carlson, C. W., et al. (2001). *Space Science Reviews*, 98, 33–66.

## Summary

Instrument description paper for the electron and ion plasma analyzer on the Fast Auroral Snapshot (FAST) satellite (launched 1996, apogee 4200 km, 83° inclination). The instrument suite consists of 16 Top Hat electrostatic analyzers in four stacks arranged around the spacecraft deck, providing a 360° unobstructed field of view in the spin plane at sub-second time resolution. Designed to measure pitch-angle distributions of suprathermal auroral electrons and ions with high sensitivity across the auroral acceleration region — the primary target region for understanding auroral electron beam acceleration, ion conics, and wave-particle interactions.

## Key Claims

1. **360° instantaneous FOV** in the spin plane: eight analyzer pairs on four stacks; each pair covers 180°; the pairs sum to 360° radial coverage independent of spin period.
2. **Two spectrometer functions:** four analyzers operate as electron/ion spectrometers (48 energies at 32 angles every 78 ms; 4 eV–32 keV electrons, 3 eV–24 keV ions); the remaining twelve operate as a high-time-resolution pitch-angle spectrograph (16 angles × 16 or 6 energies every 1.63 ms).
3. **Energy-angle resolution:** $\Delta E/E \sim 5\%$, angular resolution ~1.4°, sufficient to resolve narrow field-aligned beams and angular structure of ion conics.
4. **Burst mode data** triggered on-board by high electron energy flux; captures full pitch-angle and energy distributions at full time resolution during active auroral crossings.
5. **Initial flight results** confirm detection of counterstreaming electron beams, suprathermal bursts, ion conics, and auroral kilometric radiation emissions.

## Methods/Data

- Instrument type: Top Hat electrostatic analyzer (ESA) with microchannel plate (MCP) electron multiplier and discrete anode array.
- Deflector plates at ESA entrance resolve field-aligned beams to within 10° of spin plane.
- Central IDPU controls all FAST instruments and creates slow survey vs. fast burst data products.
- Platform: FAST satellite, $83°$ inclination, 350–4200 km altitude; auroral zone crossings at full coverage.
