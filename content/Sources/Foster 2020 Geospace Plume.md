# Foster et al. 2020 — Multi-Point Observations of the Geospace Plume

## Summary

Book chapter presenting a unified picture of the **geospace plume** — the redistribution of cold ($\leq 2$ eV) plasma of ionospheric origin through the coupled ionosphere–plasmasphere–magnetosphere system during major geomagnetic disturbances. Using simultaneous ground-based and space-based observations of the 17 March 2015 storm ($\text{Sym-H}_\text{min} \approx -220$ nT), the study traces the geospace plume continuously from its sub-auroral ionospheric source (SED), through the inner magnetosphere (plasmasphere erosion), to the dayside magnetopause where cold plume plasma participates in magnetic reconnection. Instruments used: Millstone Hill ISR, global GPS TEC, [[DMSP]], Van Allen Probes (RBSP), and THEMIS spacecraft.

## Key claims

- The **geospace plume** is a single continuous phenomenon with different names in different regions: (1) Storm-Enhanced Density (SED) / sub-auroral ionospheric trough at ionospheric altitudes; (2) plasmaspheric drainage plume/tail at inner magnetospheric heights; (3) cold plasma population at the magnetopause. All are manifestations of the same plasma redistribution.
- The source of the geospace plume is the **SAPS (Sub-Auroral Polarization Stream)**: during disturbed conditions, strong poleward electric fields at the outer edge of the energetic ring current drive Pedersen closure currents equatorward through the low-conductivity sub-auroral ionosphere, producing a sub-auroral electric field of ~10 mV/m radially outward in the magnetosphere. This sunward-advecting field erodes the outer plasmasphere and drives the SAPS flow channel.
- Ion flux to the cusp at 500 km altitude exceeds $3 \times 10^{14}$ m$^{-2}$ s$^{-1}$ during active plume transport; sunward topside O$^+$ flux in the SAPS/SED region reaches $\sim 2.5 \times 10^{14}$ m$^{-2}$ s$^{-1}$.
- At the dayside magnetopause, THEMIS-A observed cold plasma densities $\sim 100$ cm$^{-3}$ in the plume and warm plasma $\sim 60$ cm$^{-3}$ in the magnetosheath beyond reconnection sites; plume ions were accelerated up to ~1 keV in reconnection exhaust jets. Cold dense plume plasma arriving at the magnetopause thus modifies dayside reconnection.
- At 5.5 $R_E$ midnight sector, Van Allen Probe observations of cold TOI plasma embedded in high-altitude field lines confirm the anti-sunward transport of geospace plume plasma directly from the polar cap to the magnetotail.
- Plasmaspheric plume densities in the outer magnetosphere are typically 10–100 cm$^{-3}$; fluxes $10^{25}$–$10^{27}$ ions/s; about half the outer plasmasphere is drained to the magnetopause in the first 20 hours of a storm.
- The cold plume plasma in the PBL (plasmasphere boundary layer) modulates wave-particle interactions affecting outer radiation belt electron dynamics (loss and energization).

## Methods / data

- 17 March 2015 event: Dst/SYM-H minimum $\approx -220$ nT; IMF $B_z$ strongly southward ($\sim -20$ nT) from ~1200 UT; solar wind dynamic pressure >30 nPa; Dst decaying for ~11 hours during main phase.
- Ground: Millstone Hill ISR (azimuth scans at 500 km); global GPS TEC (Madrigal/Rideout & Coster 2006; Tsyganenko 2004 field model for equatorial projection)
- LEO: DMSP F-16 (840 km altitude; in-situ density and drift)
- Inner magnetosphere: Van Allen Probe A (RBSP-A) at 3 $R_E$, 18 MLT; HOPE instrument for O$^+$ ions; EFW for electric field
- Dayside magnetosphere: THEMIS-A, E, D spacecraft (4–7 $R_E$ noon sector); electrostatic analyzer (ESA) for cold plasma

## Open questions

- How does the geospace plume evolve during CH/HSS-driven storms (without strong southward $B_z$) as in the [[Lundquist Varney 2026]] L-type events?
- What fraction of the O$^+$ delivered to the cusp ultimately enters the ring current vs escapes as the polar wind?
- Does cold plume plasma at the magnetopause suppress or enhance reconnection rate, and what are the conditions for each?
