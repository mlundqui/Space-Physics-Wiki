# Gerard 1980 Optical F-region Processes

*A Review of Optical F-Region Processes in the Polar Atmosphere.* Gérard, J.-C. (1980). In C.S. Deehr & J.A. Holtet (Eds.), *Exploration of the Polar Upper Atmosphere*, pp. 175–187. Dordrecht: D. Reidel Publishing Company.

## Summary

Early foundational review of the excitation and emission mechanisms for key F-region optical lines in the polar ionosphere, covering O⁺(²P), O⁺(²D), N(²D), N(²P), and O(¹D). Reviews quantum yields and quenching coefficients from Atmosphere Explorer C (AE-C) satellite experiments, and discusses the role of neutral wind and ExB drift transport in controlling N(²D) horizontal morphology.

## Key Claims

1. **Multiple excitation pathways** contribute to polar F-region emissions beyond direct precipitating-particle ionization: O⁺ excited by charge exchange with N₂, dissociative recombination of O₂⁺, Penning ionization — indirect mechanisms can dominate the primary particle input.
2. **O(¹D) 630 nm emission** is excited primarily by dissociative recombination of O₂⁺ in the nighttime F region and by thermal electron impact (quenching by O and N₂) in the daytime ionosphere.
3. **O⁺(²D) and O⁺(²P)** states have been measured by AE-C; their quantum yields and quenching constants derived from these data are tabulated. Transitions from these states contribute to the red (630, 636 nm) and doublet oxygen lines.
4. **N(²D)** transport by neutral winds and ExB drifts produces significant horizontal morphological structure — model calculations show this displacement can be hundreds of kilometers, complicating direct use of N(²D) emission as a local ionization diagnostic.
5. **Quenching by O** at F-region altitudes is critical for determining whether excited states radiate before collisional de-excitation.

## Methods/Data

Review. Primarily draws on AE-C (Atmosphere Explorer C) in-situ data for O⁺ state densities and ground-based optical measurements. Theoretical calculations of N(²D) horizontal transport using modeled neutral wind and convection electric fields.

## Open Questions

- Quantitative partition between recombination-driven and precipitation-driven 630 nm emission in non-storm-time polar cap patches.
- Transport effects on 630 nm (not explicitly treated in this early review; addressed later by Perry & St.-Maurice 2018 for altitude effects and Sojka et al. 1997 for altitude redistribution).
