# Kletzig 2013 EMFISIS

**Citation:** Kletzing, C. A., et al. (2013), *The Electric and Magnetic Field Instrument Suite and Integrated Science (EMFISIS) on RBSP*, Space Sci. Rev., 179, 127–181, doi:10.1007/s11214-013-9993-6.

## Summary

Instrument description paper for EMFISIS on the NASA Radiation Belt Storm Probes (RBSP; now Van Allen Probes), launched August 30, 2012. EMFISIS comprises three main elements: (1) a DC fluxgate magnetometer (MAG) for background B; (2) the Waves instrument Waveform Receiver (WFR) measuring full-vector E and B from 10 Hz to 12 kHz; (3) the High Frequency Receiver (HFR) measuring a single electric field component from 10 kHz to 500 kHz. The three primary science objectives are radiation belt enhancement mechanisms, relativistic electron loss mechanisms, and the influence of the ring current and other geomagnetic processes on radiation belt behavior.

## Key claims

- WFR provides the first full 3-D vector wave measurements (both E and B) in the inner magnetosphere, enabling wave-normal analysis (WNA) that yields polarization, ellipticity, and Poynting flux — not achievable with prior single-axis instruments.
- HFR upper hybrid line enables electron density estimates independent of and often superior to particle instruments, especially at high densities where particle detectors saturate.
- Five data levels: L0 (raw telemetry), L1 (time-tagged), L2 (physical units, used for science), L3 (inertial coordinate transforms), L4 (WNA and plasma density derived products).
- Key observational targets: ULF waves (radial diffusion), chorus (local acceleration, loss), EMIC (relativistic electron precipitation), hiss (slot region formation), magnetosonic/equatorial noise.
- Over 500 refereed papers resulted from EMFISIS data in the subsequent decade ([[Kletzig 2023 EMFISIS Science]]).

## Methods/data

- MAG: triaxial fluxgate, DC to ~10 Hz, 64 vectors/s; calibrated per [[Vasquez 2020 Van Allen Probe FGM Calibration]].
- WFR: selectable sampling rates; burst mode for full-resolution captures during targeted events.
- HFR: spectral surveys every ~6 s across 10 kHz–500 kHz.
